# Point-by-point response to reviewers

**Manuscript:** ARRAY-D-26-04165  
**Title:** APG-AMS: Entropy-Guided Screening for Adaptive Metric Search in Distance-Based Learning

We thank the Editor and both reviewers for their constructive comments. In response, we substantially expanded the empirical evaluation while preserving the paper's original theoretical scope. The revised computational artifact now evaluates five methods on a common 10-fold stratified cross-validation protocol over 12 datasets and reports predictive performance, end-to-end runtime, APG-AMS search reduction, fold-level outputs, and paired statistical tests. All new numerical values are generated by the public reproducibility workflow in this repository.

## Reviewer 1

**Comment:**  
“This was a short paper whose main idea is to find the best distance metric using entropy. They state that their goal and results do not replace the successes of classical distance measures, instead, that they have a speed up. I approve of this paper. However, I believe that they should have more evidence of their success by more indepth comparisons of different datasets and with different algorithms.”

**Response:**  
We thank the reviewer for the positive assessment and for requesting stronger empirical evidence. We substantially expanded the evaluation in two directions.

First, we increased the evaluation suite from eight to twelve datasets. In addition to the original Iris, Wine, Breast Cancer, Digits, Synthetic-20D, Synthetic-50D, Two Moons, and Concentric Circles datasets, we added four real-world UCI datasets: Ionosphere, Sonar, Banknote Authentication, and Mice Protein Expression. The added datasets broaden the evaluation across signal, image-derived, and biological measurements and include higher-dimensional real-world settings such as Sonar (60 features) and Mice Protein Expression (77 usable feature columns in the implemented classification table).

Second, we expanded the algorithmic comparison from Euclidean k-NN and full adaptive p-search to five methods evaluated on identical outer stratified folds: Euclidean k-NN, full adaptive p-search, APG-AMS, Neighborhood Components Analysis (NCA) followed by k-NN, and Large Margin Nearest Neighbor (LMNN) followed by k-NN. Training-fold-only preprocessing is used for all fitted transformations. We report accuracy, weighted F1, end-to-end fold runtime, APG-AMS search reduction, and paired t-test/Wilcoxon comparisons across 120 matched dataset-fold units.

The expanded experiment shows that APG-AMS retains essentially the same mean predictive performance as Euclidean k-NN: mean accuracy 0.92667 versus 0.92596 (paired t-test p = 0.32582; Wilcoxon p = 0.51178). At the same time, APG-AMS reduces candidate metric-search evaluations by 66.47% on average across the 12 dataset summaries. These additions provide the requested deeper comparison across both datasets and algorithms.

**Changes made in the revision:**
- Expanded the benchmark suite from 8 to 12 datasets.
- Added NCA and LMNN as established supervised metric-learning baselines.
- Evaluated all methods using common 10-fold stratified folds.
- Added accuracy, weighted F1, end-to-end runtime, and search-reduction reporting.
- Added paired statistical comparisons over 120 matched dataset-fold units.
- Added a public, automated reproducibility workflow and generated fold-level result files and figures.

## Reviewer 2

**Comment:**  
“The article introduces Arithmetic Power Geometry Adaptive Metric Selection (APG-AMS), an entropy-guided screening framework for reducing unnecessary adaptive metric search... The article is, in general well written, and the idea novel... the contribution computational rather than improved classification accuracy... more material needs to be added... This material can be either extra applications or direct comparison with established metric-learning methods... the experimental evaluation would benefit substantially from additional real-world applications... In particular, applications involving large-scale similarity search, retrieval, or high-dimensional data. Hence, I propose major revision.”

**Response:**  
We thank the reviewer for identifying the central empirical gap. We followed the reviewer's explicitly suggested direct-comparison route and added two established supervised metric-learning methods: Neighborhood Components Analysis (NCA) and Large Margin Nearest Neighbor (LMNN). NCA and LMNN were trained only on each training fold and evaluated on the same outer 10-fold stratified splits used by APG-AMS, Euclidean k-NN, and full adaptive p-search. We also added four real-world UCI datasets, including higher-dimensional Sonar and Mice Protein Expression data, to broaden the conditions under which the screening mechanism is evaluated.

The expanded results sharpen the interpretation of the contribution. APG-AMS is not presented as an accuracy replacement for supervised metric learning. Across the 120 matched dataset-fold units, mean accuracy was 0.92667 for APG-AMS, 0.92596 for Euclidean k-NN, 0.93091 for full adaptive p-search, 0.94431 for NCA, and 0.93338 for LMNN. NCA and LMNN therefore achieve higher average predictive accuracy than APG-AMS in this expanded evaluation, and we now state this explicitly rather than implying predictive superiority. The mean APG-AMS search reduction across the 12 dataset summaries is 66.47%, which supports the intended computational-screening interpretation.

The statistical analysis is also revised. APG-AMS remains statistically indistinguishable from Euclidean k-NN in accuracy (paired t-test p = 0.32582; Wilcoxon p = 0.51178). Relative to full adaptive p-search, APG-AMS has a small mean accuracy difference of -0.00425 (approximately -0.42 percentage points), but the difference is statistically detectable across the 120 paired folds (paired t-test p = 0.000616; Wilcoxon p = 0.000752). NCA and LMNN also have statistically higher average predictive performance than APG-AMS. We therefore removed the earlier statement that APG-AMS is statistically indistinguishable from full adaptive p-search and reframed the result as an explicit computation–accuracy trade-off.

This distinction is important: NCA and LMNN learn supervised transformations, whereas APG-AMS decides when an otherwise expensive local p-search can be skipped. The revised paper therefore compares these methods empirically without claiming that they solve the same optimization problem. The real-world additions strengthen the evidence in higher-dimensional settings, while a genuinely large-scale retrieval benchmark remains an appropriate direction for future work; we do not claim to have performed one in this revision.

**Changes made in the revision:**
- Added direct NCA and LMNN comparisons, as specifically suggested by the reviewer.
- Added four real-world datasets and retained the high-dimensional controlled stress tests.
- Added common-fold predictive and end-to-end runtime comparisons.
- Added 120-pair statistical comparisons.
- Corrected the manuscript's earlier claim regarding equivalence to full adaptive p-search.
- Reframed APG-AMS explicitly as a computational screening method rather than an accuracy replacement for supervised metric learning.
- Revised the limitations to acknowledge that a genuinely large-scale retrieval application remains future work.
- Added a public reproducibility repository with pinned dependencies, automated tests, generated CSV outputs, and figures.
